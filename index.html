Privacy policy for Press Pulse Log

Last updated 20 October 2026.

Press Pulse Log is a personal health log. Privacy is of the utmost importance to us, and we take that responsibility very seriously. This page explains what the app processes, why, who can see it, how long it is kept, and your rights.

How we protect your privacy

The app is built on data minimisation and privacy by design (GDPR Article 25). We only ask for an email address. We do not ask for your name, or for other identifying details. Your email address is stored only in Supabase’s separate sign-in system, not in the tables that hold the log. Supabase may log sign-in events. The log is linked only to a random account ID, which makes those entries pseudonymous. The health entries are end-to-end encrypted, so we cannot read them where they are stored. Your email address identifies the account.

2. Signing in

There is no password. You first sign in with a one-time code sent to your email through Resend. After that, you can add a passkey and use it later. A passkey is unlocked with the same thing you use to unlock your phone, such as Face ID, Touch ID, or your device passcode. You do not have to add one. If you add one, Supabase stores the public key. We strongly recommend creating a passkey and saving it in iCloud Keychain or a password manager, so signing in later is quick and easy, without waiting for an email code. The private key stays in iCloud Keychain or the password manager, and Supabase keeps only the public key.

3. End-to-end encrypted data

Supabase stores the log in Ireland. Each row is sealed on the phone before it is uploaded. The encryption is AES-GCM, with a 256-bit key created on the phone. The key is kept in iCloud Keychain and in the QR share code. Using the key requires Face ID, Touch ID, or your device passcode. The key is never sent to Supabase, so these entries cannot be read there. The stored tables keep only the row id, the log id, and the ciphertext. The owner can change the entries in the app. Security describes the key, and Who can see it describes a shared log.

  • Medicines. Name, unit, comment, source, and pill shape and colour.
  • Doses. Medicine, day, and amount.
  • Phases. The phases, and which phase each day belongs to.
  • Calendar appointments. Day, time, title, kind, and note.
  • GKI readings. Day, time, value, and note.
  • Pulse sessions. Including HBOT, IV-C, EWOT, chemotherapy, and radiation. Day, kind, amount, duration, and time.
  • Lab results (blood tests and markers). Day, marker, value, unit, label, and reference range.

A few details on these rows are not encrypted: the row id, which log the row belongs to, the number of rows, and the size of the ciphertext. The table itself shows the category, for example that lab rows exist. The rows have no timestamp of their own. A time you type in, such as an appointment time, is inside the encrypted entry.

4. Data that is not end-to-end encrypted

These are stored or handled in a form the service can read. They do not include the text of the encrypted log.

  • Email address. Kept only in Supabase’s separate sign-in system, never in the tables that hold your log, so you can sign in. Resend also receives it to deliver the one-time code. It is included when you download your own data. Supabase may log sign-in events.
  • Random account ID. This links your account, your logs, and the records below. From app version 1.1 it is also the app user id sent to RevenueCat.
  • Log ownership and membership. Who owns a log or has joined it, the role, and when it was created, so the app knows who may open it.
  • A checksum of the share secret. Supabase stores only a SHA-256 hash of the secret, so a scanned code can be matched. The key and the secret themselves are never stored.
  • Phase palette colours. Stored for display only.
  • Health-data consent. The policy version (2026-10-17), your confirmation that you are an adult, and the time, so there is a record of that agreement. If you agree again, that record is replaced.
  • A record for each lab photo you send. It keeps the consent, the policy version, and the time the photo was used. It has no photo and no email. It also enforces a daily limit of 20 sends in 24 hours, and it is kept until the account is deleted.
  • Passkey public key and sign-in session data. The public key is stored only if you add a passkey, so you can sign in with it later. IP addresses are removed from sessions and from audit records.
  • Subscription status, from app version 1.1. Handled with RevenueCat and Apple. RevenueCat receives only the account ID and the App Store receipt, not your email, to determine access to paid features. Service providers explains that check.
  • The lab photo, when you choose to interpret one. The cropped image is re-drawn on the phone and sent to xAI with no account ID, no email, and no other log data. It is not stored. The reading comes back to the phone and is encrypted only when you save it. You confirm that the name and ID number are cropped out, and you agree to send it, before it goes.

5. Other data

The app has no analytics, ads, tracking, or crash-reporting software. Supabase function logs contain no user id, no email, and no content.

Language, appearance, and chart settings stay on this phone. The sign-in session stays in the keychain on the phone. The encryption key stays in iCloud Keychain and in the share code, not at Supabase. The key for xAI is a secret at Supabase. It is not on the phone.

Feedback opens the Mail app. What you send is the note you write, plus the app version, the iOS version, and the language. The log is not attached.

6. Health data (explicit consent)

Press Pulse Log is for adults. Before the log opens you confirm that you are 18 or older, and you agree that the log is health data.

Health data in the log is used because you give explicit consent, under GDPR Article 9(2)(a) and Article 6(1)(a).

The app does not give medical advice. It is a personal log.

7. Purposes

The log is stored because you agreed. The email is stored so you can sign in to that account. The email address and the account are used to provide the service you asked for, under GDPR Article 6(1)(b).

The one-time code is emailed so you can sign in. A lab photo is sent for interpretation only after you confirm that the name and ID number are cropped out, and you agree to send it. The short record of that send is used to limit sends to 20 in 24 hours.

From app version 1.1, the account ID and the App Store receipt are used to determine access to paid features. Before a lab photo is sent for interpretation, the server checks that status with RevenueCat. RevenueCat does not receive your email.

Signing out, or deleting the app, removes the session from the phone and leaves the encrypted log at Supabase.

8. Who can see it

The owner can change the entries in the app.

To share a log, the owner shows a QR code or a text code. The code contains the key that decrypts the log. Anyone with the code can read the log. Supabase stores only a SHA-256 hash of the share secret, so a scanned code can be matched. The key and the secret are never stored.

A person who joins can read the whole log, including medicines, doses, phases, appointments, GKI readings, pulse sessions (including chemotherapy and radiation), and lab results, and cannot change it. A new code stops new people from joining with the old code. Phones that already have the key can still read.

A person who only reads a shared log does not pay.

9. Service providers

These processors handle data for the app.

Supabase

Supabase, in the EU (Ireland), stores the database and runs the function that forwards a lab photo. The encrypted log is stored in Ireland. Your email address is kept in Supabase’s separate sign-in system, not in the tables that hold the log. Supabase may log sign-in events. Supabase describes its own handling in the Supabase privacy policy.

Resend

The one-time code is emailed through Resend, which receives the address only to deliver that message. Resend, in the United States, delivers the one-time email code. Resend processes and stores that address in the United States. That is where its main processing takes place. Resend can also send a message from Ireland. That only changes the route of the message. It does not store the address in the EU.

The email address is transferred to Resend in the United States, outside the EEA. The owner has accepted Resend’s data processing agreement. For this transfer, that agreement includes the EU standard contractual clauses. Resend (Plus Five Five, Inc.) is also listed as an active participant in the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. That listing is under re-certification review. The agreement is the Resend data processing addendum. The listing is on the Data Privacy Framework list. Resend describes its own handling in the Resend privacy policy.

xAI

xAI, in the USA, reads a lab photo when you send one. Lab photos are transferred to xAI at api.x.ai in the USA, outside the EEA. The owner has accepted xAI’s data processing agreement. For this transfer, that agreement includes the EU standard contractual clauses. The agreement is published as the xAI data processing addendum. What happens to the photo is described under AI. xAI describes its own handling in the xAI privacy policy.

Apple

Apple handles subscription payments and iCloud Keychain, where the encryption key is kept. Apple describes its own handling in the Apple privacy policy.

Press Pulse Log has a monthly subscription. It renews automatically and includes a 7-day free trial. Apple sells and handles the payment. We do not receive your payment details. You cancel in App Store settings. Cancelling does not delete the log.

Until app version 1.1, the app only checks on the phone whether the subscription is active, and nothing about the payment is sent to our servers.

RevenueCat

From app version 1.1, RevenueCat, Inc., in the United States, is a service provider for subscription management. It receives only the app’s random account ID (the Supabase user ID) and the App Store receipt, not your email, to determine access to paid features. Before a lab photo is sent for interpretation, the server checks that status with RevenueCat. The photo is not sent to RevenueCat.

The account ID and the App Store receipt are transferred to RevenueCat in the United States, outside the EEA. For this transfer, RevenueCat’s data processing addendum includes the EU standard contractual clauses. RevenueCat’s privacy policy says customer data sent to RevenueCat is stored on Amazon Web Services in the United States. The addendum is the RevenueCat data processing addendum. RevenueCat describes its own handling in the RevenueCat privacy policy.

10. Photos and files

A lab photo is taken with the camera. The app does not import a photo from the library, and it does not import a PDF. You crop the photo. The cropped image is re-drawn on the phone as a JPEG or PNG. You confirm that the name and ID number are cropped out, and you agree to send it.

We do not store the photo. Feedback opens the Mail app. What you send is the note you write, plus the app version, the iOS version, and the language. The log is not attached.

11. AI

The cropped photo is re-drawn on the phone, then sent to a Supabase function called interpret-lab, and from there to xAI at api.x.ai in the USA, outside the EEA. This happens only after those two confirmations. It is sent with no account ID, no email, and no other log data. The request sets store to false, which tells xAI not to store the photo. The xAI account is set to Zero Data Retention. We do not store the photo, and xAI does not store it. The reading comes back to the phone. Nothing from a reading is saved to the log until you approve the draft, and it is encrypted only when you save it.

From app version 1.1, the server checks the subscription status with RevenueCat before the photo is sent. That check uses the account ID and the App Store receipt. The photo is not sent to RevenueCat.

xAI describes its own handling in the xAI privacy policy.

12. Security

Medicines, doses, phases, calendar appointments, GKI readings, pulse sessions, and lab results are sealed on the phone before they are sent. The encryption is AES-GCM with a 256-bit key created on the phone. The key is kept in iCloud Keychain and in the QR share code. Using the key requires Face ID, Touch ID, or your device passcode. The owner shows that code as a QR code or a text code. The key is never sent to Supabase, so those entries cannot be read there. The stored rows keep the row id, the log id, and the ciphertext. The rows have no timestamp of their own. Your email, phase palette colours, and membership are not encrypted. The full lists are under End-to-end encrypted data and Data that is not end-to-end encrypted.

Supabase stores only a SHA-256 hash of the share secret, not the secret and not the key. The sign-in session stays in the keychain on the phone. If you add a passkey, Supabase stores the public key. IP addresses are removed from sessions and from audit records. Supabase may log sign-in events. The key for xAI is a secret at Supabase. It is not on the phone.

13. Retention

The log stays until you delete it. Deleting removes the rows at once. No backup copy is kept.

If you agree again, the record of that agreement is replaced. The record added for each lab photo you send is kept until the account is deleted. We do not store the photo, and xAI does not store it.

Signing out, or deleting the app, removes the session from the phone and leaves the encrypted log at Supabase.

14. Your rights

You can ask for access, a portable copy, correction, or erasure. In Settings you can download a file of the log you can see, including the sign-in email. You can download your consent.

You withdraw consent by choosing Delete my data in the app.

You can complain to the Norwegian Data Protection Authority, Datatilsynet.

15. Deleting your account

You can delete your data. That removes the sign-in email and the agreement. A log you own is deleted for everyone in it. A log you only joined stays for the others.

If you only joined, you can leave the log. It stays for the others. Leaving deletes your sign-in and your email.

Deleting removes the rows at once. No backup copy is kept. Cancelling the subscription does not delete the log. Signing out, or deleting the app, removes the session from the phone and leaves the encrypted log at Supabase.
