Privacy policy for Press Pulse Log

Last updated 20 October 2026.

On this page, “we” and “us” mean Øyvind Elgvin, who runs Press Pulse Log.

Press Pulse Log is a personal health log. Privacy is of the utmost importance to us, and we take that responsibility very seriously. This page explains what the app processes, why, who can see it, how long it is kept, and your rights.

How we protect your privacy

The app is built to collect as little as it needs, and to protect privacy from the start (data minimisation and privacy by design, GDPR Article 25). We only ask for an email address. We do not ask for your name, or for other identifying details. Your email address is stored only in Supabase’s separate sign-in system, not in the tables that hold the log. The log is linked only to a random account ID. The health entries are end-to-end encrypted, which means they are encrypted on your phone before they are sent and stored in the database, so we cannot read them where they are stored. Your email address identifies the account.

1. Data controller

Press Pulse Log is currently run by Øyvind Elgvin as a private individual. He is the data controller, which means he decides how and why your personal data is processed and is responsible for it under the GDPR. He is also responsible for this page.

2. Signing in

There is no password. You first sign in with a one-time code sent to your email through Resend. After that, you can add a passkey and use it later. A passkey is unlocked with the same thing you use to unlock your phone, such as Face ID, Touch ID, or your device passcode. You do not have to add one. If you add one, Supabase stores the public key.

We strongly recommend creating a passkey and saving it in iCloud Keychain or a password manager, so signing in later is quick and easy, without waiting for an email code. The private key stays with you, in iCloud Keychain or the password manager. Supabase keeps only the public key, which is not enough to sign in by itself.

3. End-to-end encrypted data

  • Medicines. Name, unit, comment, source, and pill shape and colour.
  • Doses. Medicine, day, and amount.
  • Phases. The phases, and which phase each day belongs to.
  • Calendar appointments. Day, time, title, kind, and note.
  • GKI readings (glucose ketone index). Day, time, value, and note.
  • Pulse sessions. Including hyperbaric oxygen (HBOT), intravenous vitamin C (IV-C), exercise with oxygen (EWOT), chemotherapy, and radiation. Day, kind, amount, duration, and time.
  • Lab results (blood tests and markers). Day, marker, value, unit, label, and reference range.

4. How encryption works

Supabase stores the log in Ireland. Each row is encrypted on your phone before it is sent and stored in the database. The method is called AES-GCM. The key that decrypts your log is created on your phone. It is stored in your iCloud Keychain and inside the QR share code. The key is never sent to Supabase or to the app’s developer, Øyvind Elgvin, who is the data controller. Using the key requires Face ID, Touch ID, or your device passcode. These entries cannot be read at Supabase. What is stored is only an id for the row, an id for the log, and the encrypted contents. The person who created the log can change its entries. People who joined with the share code can only read them. Who can see it describes a shared log.

A few details on these rows are not encrypted: the row’s id, which log the row belongs to, the number of rows, and how large the encrypted contents are. The table itself shows the category, for example that lab rows exist. The rows have no time of their own recorded beside them. A time you type in, such as an appointment time, is inside the encrypted entry.

The database uses row-level security. A signed-in user can only reach the rows of logs they created or have joined. Nobody can read another person’s log through the database. Those rows are encrypted anyway.

5. Data that is not end-to-end encrypted

These are stored or handled in a form the service can read. They do not include the text of the encrypted log.

  • Email address. Kept only in Supabase’s separate sign-in system, never in the tables that hold your log, so you can sign in. Resend also receives it to deliver the one-time code. It is included when you download your own data. Sign-in logs are described under Logging.
  • Random account ID. This links your account, your logs, and the records below. That same ID is the one sent to RevenueCat.
  • Who created a log, and who has joined it. The app stores the role and when the log was created, so it knows who may open it. The person who created the log can change it. Someone who joined can only read it.
  • A one-way fingerprint of the share secret. The QR share code contains a random secret that lets someone join your log. Supabase stores only a one-way fingerprint (a SHA-256 hash) of it, so the code itself cannot be recovered from the database. The key and the secret themselves are never stored.
  • Phase colours. Stored only so each phase can be shown in its colour.
  • Health-data consent. The policy version (2026-10-17), your confirmation that you are an adult, and the time, so there is a record of that agreement. If you accept an updated version of the consent, for example after the privacy terms change, that record is replaced and only the latest consent is kept.
  • A record for each photo of your blood test results (a lab photo) you send:
    • The consent you gave and the policy version.
    • The time the photo was used.
    • No photo and no email.
    • Used to enforce a limit of 20 sends in 24 hours.
    • Kept until the account is deleted.
  • Passkey public key and sign-in session data. The public key is stored only if you add a passkey, so you can sign in with it later. IP addresses are removed from sign-in sessions and from the records of those sign-ins.
  • Subscription status. Handled with RevenueCat and Apple. RevenueCat receives only the account ID and the App Store receipt, not your email, to determine access to paid features. Service providers explains that check.
  • A photo of your blood test results, when you choose to have it interpreted. The cropped image is re-drawn on the phone and sent to xAI with no account ID, no email, and no other log data. It is not stored. The blood test values are sent back to your phone, and when you save them they are encrypted like the rest of your log. You confirm that the name and ID number are cropped out, and you agree to send it, before it goes.

6. Other data

Language, appearance, and chart settings stay on this phone. The sign-in session stays in the phone’s secure storage (the keychain). The key that decrypts your log is created on your phone. It is stored in your iCloud Keychain and inside the QR share code. The key is never sent to Supabase or to the app’s developer, Øyvind Elgvin, who is the data controller. The separate key the app uses to reach xAI is kept at Supabase. It is not on the phone.

7. Logging

The app has no analytics, ads, tracking, or crash-reporting software.

Like any hosting provider, Supabase keeps short-lived technical logs of sign-in events for security and troubleshooting. We cannot turn these off, we only use them to fix problems, and Supabase deletes them after a short period.

The logs from Supabase’s server tasks (Edge Functions) contain no account ID, no email, and no content.

IP addresses are removed from sign-in sessions and from the records of those sign-ins.

8. Health data (explicit consent)

Press Pulse Log is for adults. Before the log opens you confirm that you are 18 or older, and you agree that the log is health data.

Health data in the log is used because you give explicit consent, under GDPR Article 9(2)(a) and Article 6(1)(a).

The app does not give medical advice. It is a personal log.

9. Purposes

Press Pulse Log is here to help you keep track of your health data during a difficult period. That includes doses, treatments, readings, and blood tests.

Some data is used only to support that: so you can sign in, share a log with people you choose, optionally have a photo of your blood test results interpreted, manage a subscription, and contact support. Your email address and account are used to provide the service you asked for, under GDPR Article 6(1)(b). Health data is covered under Health data.

Your data is never sold. It is not used for ads or tracking.

10. Who can see it

The person who created the log is the user who signs in and enters the health data. That person can change the entries. People who join with the share code are members. They can only read the log. Your log belongs to the person who created it. Øyvind Elgvin, the data controller, cannot read the encrypted entries.

To share a log, the person who created it shows a QR code or a text code. The code contains the key that opens the encrypted log. Anyone with the code can read the log. The QR share code contains a random secret that lets someone join your log. Supabase stores only a one-way fingerprint (a SHA-256 hash) of that secret, so the code itself cannot be recovered from the database. The key and the secret are never stored.

A member can read the whole log, including medicines, doses, phases, appointments, GKI readings, pulse sessions (including chemotherapy and radiation), and lab results, and cannot change it. A new code stops new people from joining with the old code. Phones that already have the key can still read.

A person who only reads a shared log does not pay.

11. Service providers

These services handle data for the app.

Supabase

Supabase, in the EU (Ireland), stores the database and runs the function that forwards a photo of your blood test results (a lab photo). The encrypted log is stored in Ireland. Your email address is kept in Supabase’s separate sign-in system, not in the tables that hold the log. Like any hosting provider, Supabase keeps short-lived technical logs of sign-in events for security and troubleshooting. We cannot turn these off, we only use them to fix problems, and Supabase deletes them after a short period. Supabase describes its own handling in the Supabase privacy policy.

Resend

The one-time code is emailed through Resend, which receives the address only to deliver that message. Resend, in the United States, delivers the one-time email code. Resend processes and stores that address in the United States. That is where its main processing takes place. Resend can also send a message from Ireland. That only changes the route of the message. It does not store the address in the EU.

The email address is transferred to Resend in the United States, outside the European Economic Area. We have accepted Resend’s data processing agreement. For this transfer, that agreement includes the EU’s standard contract terms for sending personal data abroad (standard contractual clauses). Resend (Plus Five Five, Inc.) is also listed as an active participant in the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. That listing is being reviewed for renewal. The agreement is the Resend data processing addendum. The listing is on the Data Privacy Framework list. Resend describes its own handling in the Resend privacy policy.

xAI

xAI, in the USA, reads a photo of your blood test results (a lab photo) when you send one. Lab photos are transferred to xAI at api.x.ai in the USA, outside the European Economic Area. xAI processes the image under Zero Data Retention (ZDR). The request also sets store to false. The image is not stored, and it is not used for training after the response is sent. We have accepted xAI’s data processing agreement. For this transfer, that agreement includes the EU’s standard contract terms for sending personal data abroad (standard contractual clauses). The agreement is published as the xAI data processing addendum. What happens to the photo is described under AI. xAI describes its own handling in the xAI privacy policy.

Apple

Apple handles subscription payments and iCloud Keychain, where the encryption key is kept. Apple describes its own handling in the Apple privacy policy.

Press Pulse Log has a monthly subscription. It renews automatically. Payments and subscriptions are handled by Apple through the App Store. We never see your card or payment details. You manage or cancel the subscription in your Apple ID settings. Cancelling does not delete the log.

RevenueCat

RevenueCat, Inc., in the United States, is a service provider for subscription management. It receives only the app’s random account ID (the Supabase user ID) and the App Store receipt, not your email, to determine access to paid features. Before a photo of your blood test results (a lab photo) is sent for interpretation, the server checks that status with RevenueCat. The photo is not sent to RevenueCat.

The account ID and the App Store receipt are transferred to RevenueCat in the United States, outside the European Economic Area. For this transfer, RevenueCat’s data processing agreement includes the EU’s standard contract terms for sending personal data abroad (standard contractual clauses). RevenueCat’s privacy policy says customer data sent to RevenueCat is stored on Amazon Web Services in the United States. The addendum is the RevenueCat data processing addendum. RevenueCat describes its own handling in the RevenueCat privacy policy.

12. Photos and files

A photo of your blood test results (a lab photo) is taken with the camera. The app does not import a photo from the library, and it does not import a PDF. You crop the photo. The cropped image is re-drawn on the phone as a JPEG. You confirm that the name and ID number are cropped out, and you agree to send it.

We do not store the photo.

13. Feedback

Feedback opens the Mail app. What you send is the note you write, plus the app version, the iOS version, and the language. The log is not attached. It goes to support@presspulselog.com.

14. AI

A photo of your blood test results (a lab photo) is cropped and re-drawn on the phone, then sent through a Supabase task called interpret-lab, and from there to xAI at api.x.ai in the USA, outside the European Economic Area. This happens only after those two confirmations. It is sent with no account ID, no email, and no other log data. The request tells xAI not to store the photo. In that request the setting is called store: false. xAI processes the image under Zero Data Retention (ZDR), so the image is not stored or used for training after the response. We do not store the photo. The blood test values are sent back to your phone. Nothing is saved to the log until you approve the draft. The blood test values are encrypted on your phone before they are stored in the database.

The server checks the subscription status with RevenueCat before the photo is sent. That check uses the account ID and the App Store receipt. The photo is not sent to RevenueCat.

xAI describes its own handling in the xAI privacy policy.

16. Retention

The log stays until you delete it. Deleting removes the rows at once. No backup copy is kept.

The health-data consent record is replaced when you accept an updated version of the consent, for example after the privacy terms change. Only the latest consent is kept.

The record added for each blood test photo you send is kept until the account is deleted. It contains:

  • the consent you gave
  • the policy version
  • the time the photo was used

It does not contain the photo or your email. Neither we nor xAI store the photo. The same details are listed under Data that is not end-to-end encrypted.

Signing out, or deleting the app, removes the session from the phone and leaves the encrypted log at Supabase.

17. Your rights

You can ask for access, a portable copy, correction, or erasure. In Settings you can download a file of the log you can see, including the sign-in email. You can download your consent.

You withdraw consent by choosing Delete my data in the app.

You can complain to the Norwegian Data Protection Authority, Datatilsynet.

18. Deleting your account

You can delete your data. That removes the sign-in email and the agreement. A log you created is deleted for everyone in it. A log you only joined stays for the others.

If you only joined, you can leave the log. It stays for the others. Leaving deletes your sign-in and your email.

Deleting removes the rows at once. No backup copy is kept. Cancelling the subscription does not delete the log. Signing out, or deleting the app, removes the session from the phone and leaves the encrypted log at Supabase.
