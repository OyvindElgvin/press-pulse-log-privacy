Privacy for Press Pulse Log

Last updated 19 October 2026.

Øyvind Elgvin, a private individual in Norway, is responsible for Press Pulse Log and for this page. Questions go to stoyvind@gmail.com. You can also complain to the Norwegian Data Protection Authority, Datatilsynet.

Sign-in and the log

Press Pulse Log is for adults. Before the log opens you confirm that you are 18 or older, and you agree that the log is health data. There is no password. You first sign in with a one-time code sent to your email. After that, you can add a passkey and use it later. You do not have to add one. If you add one, Supabase stores the public key.

Supabase stores your email and the log in Ireland. Doses, phases, the calendar and appointments, GKI, pulse sessions, and lab results are encrypted on the phone before they are sent. The encryption is AES-GCM. The key is kept in iCloud Keychain and in a share code. The key is never sent to Supabase, so those entries cannot be read there. Your email, palette colors, and membership are not encrypted. The log includes blood tests, medicines, chemo, and radiation. The owner can change the entries in the app.

The log is stored because you agreed. The email is stored so you can sign in to that account. Supabase describes its own handling in the Supabase privacy policy.

The one-time code is emailed through Resend, which receives the address only to deliver that message. Resend describes its own handling in the Resend privacy policy.

The app does not track you and has no ads. Signing out, or deleting the app, removes the session from the phone and leaves the encrypted log at Supabase.

Short records we keep

We also keep a few short records. They do not contain the lab photo or the text of the log.

When you agree, we store that you are an adult, the consent text version 2026-10-17, and the time. If you agree again, that record is replaced.

Each lab photo you send adds a record with your user id, the consent text version, the two confirmations, the time you agreed, and the time the photo was used. It has no photo and no email. It is used to limit sends to 20 in 24 hours. It is kept until the account is deleted.

The function that sends a lab photo logs only lengths, counts, and error codes. Sign-in audit logs and sessions do not keep your IP address or your email.

What you can do in Settings

You can download a file of the log you can see, including the sign-in email. You can download your consent.

You can delete your data. That removes the sign-in email and the agreement. A log you own is deleted for everyone in it. A log you only joined stays for the others.

If you only joined, you can leave the log. It stays for the others. Leaving deletes your sign-in and your email.

Deleting removes the rows from the live database. The log otherwise stays until you delete it.

A shared log

The owner shows a QR code or a text code. The code contains the key that decrypts the log. Anyone with the code can read the log. Supabase stores only a SHA-256 checksum of the secret part of the code, not the code and not the key.

A person who joins can read the whole log, including blood tests, medicines, chemo, radiation, and GKI, and cannot change it. A new code stops new people from joining with the old code. Phones that already have the key can still read.

On this phone

Language, appearance, and zoom stay on this phone. The sign-in session stays in the keychain on the phone. The key for xAI is a secret at Supabase. It is not on the phone.

A lab photo is taken with the camera. The app does not import a photo from the library, and it does not import a PDF. You crop the photo. The cropped file is a JPEG or PNG. You confirm that the name and ID number are cropped out, and you agree to send it.

Feedback opens the Mail app. What you send is the note you write, plus the app version, the iOS version, and the language. The log is not attached.

Lab photos and xAI

The cropped photo is sent to a Supabase function called interpret-lab, and from there to xAI at api.x.ai in the USA, outside the EEA. This happens only after those two confirmations. The rest of the log is not sent. The request sets store to false, which tells xAI not to store the photo. The xAI account is set to Zero Data Retention. We do not store the photo, and xAI does not store it. Nothing from a reading is saved to the log until you approve the draft.

xAI describes its own handling in the xAI privacy policy.

Subscription

Press Pulse Log has a monthly subscription. It renews automatically and includes a 7-day free trial. Apple sells and handles the payment. The app only checks on the phone whether the subscription is active. We do not receive your payment details, and nothing about the payment is sent to our servers. A person who only reads a shared log does not pay. You cancel in App Store settings. Cancelling does not delete the log. Apple describes its own handling in the Apple privacy policy.

Health data in the log is used because you give explicit consent, under GDPR Article 9(2)(a) and Article 6(1)(a). The email address and the account are used to provide the service you asked for, under GDPR Article 6(1)(b).

You can ask for access, a portable copy, correction, or erasure. You withdraw consent by choosing Delete my data in the app. You can complain to Datatilsynet.

These processors handle data for the app. Supabase, in the EU (Ireland), stores the database and runs the function that forwards a lab photo. Resend delivers the one-time email code. xAI, in the USA, reads a lab photo when you send one. Apple handles subscription payments and iCloud Keychain, where the encryption key is kept.

Lab photos are transferred to xAI in the USA, outside the EEA. The email address is sent to Resend to deliver the code.
